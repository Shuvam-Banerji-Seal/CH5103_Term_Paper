\paragraph{Functional and separation dependence (T11c).} The same vertical test was repeated on
UB3LYP/6-31G* geometries with B3LYP and with the range-separated CAM-B3LYP, which has less
delocalisation error. The larger TEMPO-4-carboxylate was added to vary the radical--anion separation.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llrrr}
\toprule
radical & functional & IE$\to$T (eV) & IE$\to$CS (eV) & $\Delta$(T$-$CS) (eV)\\
\midrule
short anion & B3LYP     & 4.012 & 3.939 & $+0.072$\\
short anion & CAM-B3LYP & 4.182 & 4.545 & $\mathbf{-0.363}$\\
short acid  & B3LYP     & 9.498 & 8.092 & $+1.406$\\
short acid  & CAM-B3LYP & 9.670 & 8.249 & $+1.421$\\
TEMPO-4-COO$^-$ & B3LYP & 3.978 & 3.912 & $+0.067$\\
TEMPO-4-COO$^-$ & CAM-B3LYP & 4.120 & 4.416 & $\mathbf{-0.296}$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
$\ev{\hat S^2}_T=2.007$--$2.011$ throughout (negligible spin contamination). The $\alpha$-SOMO lies 3 (short
anion) or 2--3 (TEMPO anion; B3LYP/CAM-B3LYP) levels below the $\alpha$-HOMO, and is the HOMO in the acid.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item With CAM-B3LYP, oxidation of the anion gives the \textbf{triplet diradical}, 0.36~eV below the closed
        shell. The protonated acid still prefers the closed shell by 1.42~eV. \emph{The review's energetic pH
        switch is reproduced.}
  \item The B3LYP ``no crossover'' result of T11b is functional-specific. B3LYP lowers the closed-shell
        product by 0.61~eV relative to CAM-B3LYP, but the triplet by only 0.17~eV. Attributing this to
        delocalisation error is a hypothesis, not tested here.
  \item The review's own class of system behaves the same way. TEMPO-4-carboxylate gives the diradical at
        CAM-B3LYP ($-0.296$~eV) and narrowly misses it at B3LYP ($+0.067$~eV). Separation is not the deciding
        factor; the functional is. The protonated TEMPO acid (the control) was not computed: the run was
        stopped when the workstation ran out of memory.
\end{enumerate}
